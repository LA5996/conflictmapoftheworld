About and methods

Conflict Map of the World is a free, independent and non-commercial educational site. It puts the world’s armed conflicts, serious violence, natural disasters, political changes and economic ties on one map, so anyone can see what is happening and where.

Where the information comes from

Every entry is written from published reporting by news agencies and major outlets (such as AP, Reuters and AFP), UN agencies, and independent conflict monitors such as ACLED, the Institute for the Study of War / Critical Threats, the Long War Journal and Crisis Group. Each entry links to its sources so you can check it yourself. Live earthquake data comes directly from the US Geological Survey.

How it is updated

The site is refreshed every morning (Central European Time). An AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic) researches recent reporting for each section and drafts updates under fixed rules: neutral language, every claim sourced, nothing included that cannot be checked, and casualty figures only as given by the cited source. The editor reviews the site and corrects errors. We are open about this process because readers deserve to know how a source works.

How we decide what to include

  • Conflicts: ongoing armed conflicts and crises with regular deadly violence. Major war means sustained large-scale fighting with front lines and very high casualties; serious conflict means frequent deadly fighting, insurgency, or an interstate war in an uneasy pause; lower intensity means recurring violence or strikes, often under a ceasefire that is not fully holding. These are editorial groupings to help comparison, not legal classifications.
  • Day-to-day violence: significant individual incidents from recent weeks, such as mass-casualty attacks or major outbreaks of gang, cartel or communal violence.
  • Natural disasters: major recent events with serious humanitarian impact, plus live earthquakes of magnitude 5.5 and stronger.
  • Political changes: national elections, changes of leader, major court rulings and constitutional changes, and important upcoming votes.
  • Economic ties: significant trade agreements, tariff changes, sanctions and energy deals between countries or blocs.

Neutrality

We describe what happened and who is involved, using the same standards for every side. We do not take positions on conflicts, candidates or disputed territory. Maps use common international borders for orientation only.

Corrections

If something is wrong or out of date, please report it with a link to a reliable source. Confirmed errors are corrected promptly.

Limits

Situations change quickly and early reports are often revised. Markers show the main area concerned, not precise locations. This site is for general education and should not be used for safety, travel or financial decisions; for those, consult official sources.
